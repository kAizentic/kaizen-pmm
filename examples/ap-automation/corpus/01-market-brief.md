# Mid-market accounts payable: state of the function, 2026

## Scope

This brief covers companies with 200 to 2,000 employees that process between 1,500 and 15,000
supplier invoices a month. Most run a mid-market ERP and staff accounts payable with two to eight
people reporting to a controller.

## Findings

**Approval routing, not data entry, is now the bottleneck.** Capture and coding have largely been
automated by ERP add-ons and OCR. In our sample, invoices spend a median of 9.4 days waiting for an
approver and 1.6 days in data entry. The approver is usually a budget owner outside finance who
treats invoice approval as interrupt work.

**Exceptions consume most of the team's time.** Invoices that fail a match against the purchase
order or receipt make up 18% of volume but 61% of accounts-payable staff hours. Exceptions are
resolved over email, so the reasoning behind each resolution is not recorded anywhere an auditor
can see.

**Cost per invoice has stalled.** Fully loaded processing cost sits between $9 and $14 per invoice
for companies that have adopted ERP-native AP modules, against $15 to $22 for fully manual teams.
The gap between the two groups narrowed from 2024 to 2026 because the ERP modules automate capture
but leave routing and exceptions manual.

**Audit and fraud pressure is rising.** 41% of controllers surveyed said their auditors had added
testing on payment controls in the last two years. Vendor bank-detail change requests are the most
cited fraud vector.

## Alternatives buyers consider

1. **ERP-native AP modules**, such as the Contoso Finance Suite AP module. Chosen because they are
   already licensed and sit inside the system of record. Their weakness is approval routing and
   exception handling, which they treat as a simple linear workflow.
2. **Outsourced AP providers**, such as Fabrikam Payables Services. Chosen for headcount relief at
   high volume. Buyers report losing visibility into exceptions and approvals, and a slower response
   when a supplier disputes a payment.
3. **The status quo**: spreadsheets, a shared inbox and email approvals. Still the most common
   competitor, because switching requires finance to win time from IT for the ERP integration.

## Implication

The next gain in mid-market AP comes from approvals and exceptions rather than from capture. A
vendor that shortens approval wait time and leaves an audit trail on every exception addresses the
two costs that ERP-native modules have not.
