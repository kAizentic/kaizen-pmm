# Win/loss review, Q2-Q3 2026

Twelve decided opportunities: 5 won, 7 lost. Average deal size was $31,000 ARR.

## Wins (5)

- **Four of five wins were against the Contoso Finance Suite AP module**, where the buyer already
  owned Contoso. The deciding factor in each was multi-level approval routing with escalation, and
  approvals that non-ERP users could complete from email or mobile.
- Three wins cited the exception audit trail during the procurement stage, typically after the
  controller showed it to their auditor.
- Median sales cycle for wins: 47 days. Every win ran a 30-day pilot on one business unit first.

## Losses (7)

- **Status quo (4 losses).** In each case the project stalled waiting for IT to schedule the ERP
  integration. Two of the four said they would revisit after their ERP upgrade.
- **Fabrikam Payables Services (2 losses).** Both buyers had more than 12,000 invoices a month and
  chose on cost per invoice. Neither ran a pilot.
- **Contoso (1 loss).** The buyer's IT team refused a second system touching the ERP.

## Patterns

- We win when the controller or CFO owns the decision and the pain is approvals or audit. We lose
  when the pain is framed as data-entry cost, because Contoso and Fabrikam both answer that.
- Deals with a pilot closed at 5 of 6; deals without a pilot closed at 0 of 6.
- The integration effort is the most common reason deals stall. Buyers asked for a read-only ERP
  connection first, with write-back later.
