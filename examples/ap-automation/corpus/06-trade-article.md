# Auditors turn their attention to payment controls at mid-sized companies

*Mid-Market Finance Review (a fictional publication), June 2026*

Audit firms are spending more time on accounts-payable controls at companies below the enterprise
tier, according to partners at three regional firms interviewed for this article.

The shift follows a rise in business email compromise. Payment-redirection fraud, in which a
criminal impersonates a supplier and asks for bank details to be changed, now accounts for the
largest share of reported losses among mid-sized companies, according to the firms. One partner said
nearly every client in the 200-to-2,000-employee range had seen at least one attempt in the past
year.

Auditors are asking two questions more often. First, can the company show who approved each payment,
and was that person authorized for the amount? Second, is there an independent verification step
when a supplier's bank details change?

"Most of these companies have an approval process. Very few can prove it," one partner said.
"Approvals happen in email, and the evidence is a forwarded message with no link to the payment."

Controllers interviewed said the cost of reconstructing approval evidence during fieldwork is
significant. One estimated it at two weeks of a senior accountant's time per audit.

Smaller companies are responding unevenly. Some have added callback verification for bank-detail
changes; few have changed how approvals are recorded.
